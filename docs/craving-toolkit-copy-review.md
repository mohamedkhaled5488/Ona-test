# Craving toolkit: wording for review

Written for: the clinician reviewing Ona's craving toolkit before release.

Ona's Quit section offers a short "toolkit" when someone taps "I'm craving it": breathing, grounding, small absorbing tasks, movement, and prompts to reflect or reach out. Each tool shows one line headed **"Why this can help"**. These lines are drafts. Please check each one for accuracy and tone, and mark any change.

**Ground rules for the wording**

- Modest, correlational language ("can help", "many people find"). No promises, no cures, no medical advice.
- No exact durations stated as facts.
- No shame: a slip is "a hard day", never a failure.
- No chance-based or reward mechanics in any tool.

A test in the app blocks words such as "guarantee", "cure", "treatment", "withdrawal", "dose", "fail" and "reward". It can't judge accuracy; that's what this review is for.

## Questions for you

1. **Substances note.** It appears only for habits tagged "Alcohol or drugs". Should other types get it too (for example nicotine), or should benzodiazepines be named separately?
2. **Tense and release.** Should the steps carry a caution, for example for people with injuries or pain?
3. **Body scan.** Some people find attention on the body uncomfortable. Should it say "skip any part that doesn't feel right"?
4. **Breathing.** The patterns are even (4 in, 4 out), slow exhale (4 in, 6 out) and box (4 in, hold 4, 4 out, hold 4). Are these suitable, and is holding the breath appropriate for everyone?
